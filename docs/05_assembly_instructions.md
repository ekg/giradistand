# 05 — Home assembly instructions

**One person, 35–45 minutes, no power tools.** Tools: 3 mm hex key (or the hex key that
comes with most flat-pack furniture), 8 mm open-end wrench, 10 mm open-end wrench,
torque feel — "firm hand-tight, then a quarter turn".

No drilling, tapping, filing, bending, painting or welding is required *except* the two
wall fixings for the anti-tip strap (step 9), which is the only operation outside the
kit. The parts arrive cut, debent, bent, powder coated, with every hole and slot
in place and every thread in a purchased locknut.

## Sequence (checked for insertion direction and access)

**1. Lay out and identify (3 min).**
Put the two P1 slabs face-down on a rug, outer faces down. They are 400 × 612 mm.
Check that the 11 holes are visible.

**2. Feet into the base tray (3 min).**
P3 has four 6.6 mm holes near its corners. Screw one M6 glide into each from below,
then run an M6 locknut down from above and leave it loose. **Arrow/dot on the tray
face must point to the front** (the front flange is 20 mm; the rear is 30 mm).
Feet positions: 262 mm from the centre line left/right, 183 mm front/rear.

**3. Base tray onto the slabs (6 min) — two hands, no helper needed.**
Stand both slabs on edge, outer faces out, 566.2 mm apart, on the floor. Lower P3
between them so its two *end* flanges sit against the slabs' inner faces, lining up the 3 slots
per side with the 3 lowest holes in each slab. Insert 3 M5 screws per side from the
outside, add a locknut on the inside, and leave them **finger-tight**. The tray will
hang; that is expected.

**4. Turn the assembly upright (1 min).**
Two people is safer here if you are not comfortable lifting 10 kg while it is loose;
one person can do it by rolling the assembly onto the base tray. Now it is a "U".

**5. Rear panel P4 (4 min).**
Slide P4 down between the slabs at the rear: its two forward flanges sit against the
slab inner faces with the 2 holes per side aligned. 2 M5 screws per side, from the
outside, locknuts inside, finger-tight. It rests on the base tray.
*The panel is the stand's racking resistance — do not leave it out.*

**6. Middle tray P2 (6 min).**
Drop the tray in from above, flanges outside the slabs, slots over the middle holes.
3 M5 per side, finger-tight.

**7. Top tray P2 (6 min).**
Same as step 6, at the top holes. Both sides, all six screws, finger-tight.

**8. Level and torque (8 min) — the step that matters.**
1. Stand the frame on its feet.
2. Put a spirit level on the **top tray** front-to-back and left-right. Adjust the four
   M6 glides (turn the glide, not the nut) until the top tray is level within about
   1 mm per 300 mm. Re-check the base tray; if the floor is dished, split the
   difference — the top tray is what matters.
3. Now set each tray level **in its slots** and tighten. Work in this order: top tray
   first, then middle, then base, then the rear panel. Tighten the M5 locknuts to a
   firm hand-tight plus a quarter turn (≈3 N·m with a 3 mm key) while holding the
   screw head still. The slots allow ±0.65 mm of vertical float; tightening is what
   fixes each shelf's height, so tighten after levelling, not before.
4. Lock the M6 feet locknuts against the base tray underside.
5. Push the stand gently side-to-side and front-to-back: it should feel like one piece,
   with no creak and no relative movement at any corner.

**9. Anti-tip strap P5 (5 min).**
Bolt the strap's lower flange to the underside of the top tray's rear edge with one M5
screw through the tray's rear flange and a locknut (drill-free).
For the wall end, hold the strap against the wall so it is snug but **not** in tension,
mark the hole, drill a 3 mm pilot and drive the appropriate fixing for your wall
(4 × 40 wood screw into a stud, or a plasterboard anchor). This is the only hole you
drill in the whole project.
Re-check that all four feet still carry load after fixing.

**10. Speaker isolators (2 min).**
Peel the eight ISO-PUCK mini isolators into the middle tray in the four positions
marked by the small etched dots (32 mm in from the speaker footprint corners):
left pair at x = −143.6 ± 101.5, right pair at x = +143.6 ± 101.5, y = +103 ± 65.

**11. Load it (5 min).**
1. **Record crate** first (bottom): slide it in from the front; it stops against the
   rear panel. It should clear the tray above by about 11 mm.
2. **Speakers** (middle): lower each one onto its four isolators from the front, grille
   facing you, cabinet "lying down" so the wedge's slope leans back. Set them 6 mm in
   from each side and leave the 20 mm gap between them. Connect RCA/DC/AC **before**
   setting them in place if the connectors are hard to reach; 206 mm of space is left
   behind each cabinet.
3. **Turntable** (top): set it on the top tray, 57 mm of margin each side, and level the
   platter with the turntable's own feet. Remove the dust cover for playing
   (Audio-Technica's recommendation).

## Disassembly

Reverse order. Every fastener is a locknut on a screw, so nothing is destroyed:
1. Equipment off.
2. Strap off the wall.
3. Top tray, middle tray, rear panel, base tray (screws out from the outside — no
   access to an inaccessible back face is ever needed).
4. Feet out of the base tray.
The flat parts nest into about a 620 × 460 × 60 mm stack plus the slabs.

## What to check before you tighten anything

| symptom | cause | action |
|---|---|---|
| a tray will not drop into place | the two slabs are not parallel | loosen the base and rear panel, re-square, retighten |
| a screw will not start | tray flange slot not aligned to the slab hole | slide the tray vertically in its slot (that is what the ±0.65 mm is for) |
| a tray sits high at one corner | the tray is rotated 180° (harmless for P2, which is symmetric in Y, but rotate it to get the flanges the right way) | rotate and refit |
| the stand rocks | floor not level, or feet not extended | extend the low foot, re-level, lock the nut |
| creak when pushed sideways | a locknut left loose | re-torque all 22 |

**Never force a visibly misaligned joint.** Every joint here has deliberate movement in
it; if the parts do not go together, the frame is out of square, and forcing it will
pre-load the tray and spoil the turntable's level.
