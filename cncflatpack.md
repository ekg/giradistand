Act as a furniture designer and mechanical engineer experienced in CNC-routed plywood, parametric CAD, flat-pack joinery, and practical manufacturing. Design a compact three-level stand, generate its actual manufacturing files, validate the design, and prepare an upload-ready fabrication package. The goal is a kit that assembles almost like a large wooden puzzle, with approximately US$300 as the working budget.

Complete the design and available validation work. A concept image, a shopping list, or instructions for somebody else to create the CAD are insufficient. Use documented assumptions to make progress while identifying the few measurements necessary before fabrication.

1. Product requirements

- Top: an Audio-Technica turntable, with a modest usable margin around it. Keep the complete stand’s footprint as close to the turntable’s footprint as the other equipment allows.
- Middle: BOTH Acoustic Research Powered Partners lying horizontally, side by side, with grilles facing forward. Include speaker isolation and room for connectors, ventilation, and controls.
- Bottom: one removable record box, crate, or suitable holders containing upright LPs. Two separate crates are optional. Preserve practical insertion, removal, and browsing access.
- Construction: CNC-cut plywood panels with tabs and slots, plus positive locking keys, wedges, or a small number of simple furniture fasteners. Prefer an open, visually simple design with exposed plywood edges.
- Assembly: parts should arrive labeled, with safe edges and all required joinery and holes machined. No measuring, sawing, drilling, routing, chiseling, or force-fitting at home. Aim for hand assembly, optionally a rubber mallet or one ordinary driver. Specify any additional work explicitly.
- Disassembly: prefer a reversible design that can be taken apart for moving. Make a practical tool-free version if it is reliable and economical; a few accessible bolts are acceptable when they materially improve rigidity or cost.

Prioritize correct fit, stiffness and stability, easy assembly, affordable fabrication, compactness, and appearance. Keep the project proportionate to a small household furniture build.

2. Budget and scope

Use approximately US$300 as the target for the complete usable stand kit: plywood, machining, edge cleanup, necessary locks or fasteners, feet, basic speaker isolation, shipping, and tax where applicable. Exclude the audio equipment and record containers, but identify their costs separately if recommending new ones. State which costs remain unknown.

Try to keep material and CNC work around US$220–250 to leave room for the remaining items. These are design allowances, not supplier quotations. Quote optional decorative finishes separately. If a complete finished kit cannot meet the target, show the least expensive suitable option and explain the tradeoff. Do not quietly transfer substantial fabrication or cleanup to me to make the price appear lower.

Use simple geometry, economical sheet utilization, and few distinct manufacturing operations. Avoid unnecessary variants. Briefly compare credible approaches, select one, and develop it fully. Do not buy materials, place manufacturing orders, or send messages to suppliers. Prepare everything necessary for an accurate quote; report whether a live quotation was actually obtained.

4. Choose the material and upload service together

Primary approach: good-quality birch plywood around nominal 18 mm or ¾ inch, CNC-routed into flat-pack parts. Select an actual supplier product with appropriate core quality and face grade. Baltic birch or another suitable multi-ply panel may be preferred if the budget allows. State acceptable core voids and flatness for the joints and loaded shelves.

Nominal 18 mm, nominal ¾ inch, and actual measured thickness are different quantities. Confirm the specific stock and measure thickness at multiple relevant positions before production. Do not design interference fits around a nominal label.

Consider Pixels to Prototype’s online upload route as a primary candidate: its published service accepts one-off projects, lists nominal ¾-inch plywood, and accepts DXF/SVG and other formats. Its web form returns a reviewed quotation rather than an instant price. Verify current stock, plywood grade, tolerances, cleanup, finishing, and delivery. See [2] and [3].

SendCutSend is an alternative with CAD upload and instant pricing. Its previously checked plywood catalog tops out at 12 mm. If using that route, verify current stock and engineer a separate 12 mm construction with suitable reinforcement and joints. Do not simply change the thickness parameter in an 18 mm design and retain its load claims. Its routed parts may retain small manufacturing tabs; determine who removes them. See [4] and [5].

A different service is acceptable if its actual upload workflow, material, process, and delivered cost are better suited. Verify relevant capabilities and cite primary sources. Do not infer thickness availability from a generic claim that a service cuts wood.

5. Develop the assembly and dimension chains

Start by evaluating two sides, three shelves, and a deliberate rear bracing element, with additional small locking keys or structural rails only where useful. Treat this as a candidate architecture, not an arbitrary six-part limit.

Explain how each component locates, carries load, prevents withdrawal, and resists rotation. Provide a real load path from every shelf to the floor. Prefer shelf tabs or shoulders that bear appropriately on the side structure, with locks keeping joints engaged. Design the empty stand to remain together when moved by its intended handling points.

Show dimension chains for the speaker bay, record bay, top surface, and overall stand. Account for panel thickness, joint intrusions, isolation pads, connectors, handling clearance, locking keys, bracing, and production tolerances. Distinguish clear usable openings from distances between nominal shelf planes.

All turntable feet must sit fully on the top support without contacting raised keys, screw heads, or edge features. Verify dust-cover travel and wall clearance. Keep speaker grilles unobstructed and preserve access to cables and ventilation. Check the record container through its complete insertion/removal path, including loaded records and handles.

Choose and justify an operating height. Show the complete vertical stack from floor and feet through the shelf levels. Avoid unexplained empty height. Keep the visual design restrained, with a few deliberate proportions and a coherent exposed-edge treatment.

6. Design machinable, tolerant joinery

Parameterize stock thickness, measured thickness range, slot clearance, tab size, router diameter, machining tolerance, finish allowance, and locking geometry independently. Select fit allowances based on the chosen stock and process. Do not claim that one universal clearance guarantees a good fit.

Provide a small fit-test coupon containing a representative tab-and-slot joint and the proposed lock, with a practical range of clearance options. It must be cut from the production stock using the intended tool and process. Use the result to finalize joint dimensions before cutting the complete kit. Account for coating at mating surfaces if finishing occurs before assembly. Source [6] documents this general fit-testing workflow.

Use cutter-compatible internal corners: dogbone or T-bone reliefs, radiused mating corners, or another justified approach. Model the actual relief geometry and check the wood remaining around it. Avoid fragile tongues, short unsupported fingers, or narrow ligaments that can split, crush, or delaminate.

Choose removable locking wedges, keys, or economical knockdown hardware. Show how locks engage, are retained, can be removed, and maintain joint tightness. Do not rely entirely on a press fit or assume that friction alone supplies racking resistance. Avoid locks that require aggressive hammering or concentrate force into thin veneers.

If using hardware, provide exact sizes, lengths, quantities, accessible tightening directions, and all required holes or recesses. Flag edge drilling, underside machining, embedded hardware, or other operations that a simple one-face cutting service may not perform. Do not require the user to improvise these operations during assembly.

Keep finished geometry separate from cutter compensation. Export nominal part outlines with the intended fit allowances; let the agreed CAM workflow offset the cutter appropriately. Do not accidentally compensate for tool radius twice.

7. Prove that it can be assembled

Write and check the assembly sequence before finalizing joints. A collision-free final model does not prove the parts can reach their assembled positions.

For each step, identify the insertion direction, required temporary clearance, components already installed, and access for hands and any tools. Check that closed slots, opposing tabs, rear braces, and the final side or top do not trap one another. Show an exploded view and intermediate assembly states.

Make assembly practical for one person where possible. Use discreet part labels and orientation marks, including left/right or front/back where relevant. Place labels where they will not be mistaken for production cuts or spoil the visible finish.

Demonstrate disassembly as well. Locks must remain accessible after the equipment is removed. Identify intentional tight fits and prohibit forcing a visibly misaligned joint. Estimate assembly time and list the exact tools based on the checked sequence.

8. Validate plywood structure and stability

Set explicit service loads and numerical acceptance criteria before declaring success. Until actual loads are known, consider provisional payloads of:

- 15 kg on top.
- 10 kg on the middle shelf.
- 40 kg on the bottom shelf.
- Self-weight in addition to those payloads.

These are starting assumptions. Justify any revision from actual equipment and intended record capacity; do not present them as certified ratings.

Check simultaneous loading, individual shelf loading, turntable feet, speaker pads, record-box contact points, eccentric loads, and relevant handling conditions. Use realistic contact areas rather than assuming every payload is uniformly distributed.

Check shelf bending and deflection, long-term deflection under stored records, side panels, rear bracing, tab bending and shear, bearing/crushing around slots, splitting and delamination, connector loads, and feet. Identify the governing mode and justify the chosen panel thickness and reinforcement.

Use plywood-appropriate directional material properties or clearly conservative assumptions. Preserve the face-grain directions assumed in the calculations. Consider core quality and moisture-related movement. Do not apply an isotropic steel model or a generic yield-stress check and call it a plywood analysis.

Provide reproducible calculations with sources, formulas, units, boundary conditions, assumptions, margins, and results. State whether capacities are allowable or ultimate. Set justified limits for shelf sag, differential turntable-foot movement, twist, and racking. Use finite-element analysis only if available and useful, with credible material and joint modeling; transparent analytical checks are acceptable when their limits are stated.

Evaluate racking in both horizontal directions, rocking on the feet, sliding, and tipping. Use the actual support footprint and centers of mass. Include the stand with records removed, asymmetric storage, and a plausible partly withdrawn container. State any lateral disturbance used and its application height. Do not rely on stored records always acting as ballast.

Revise inadequate geometry. If a wall restraint is recommended, explain the installation implications and distinguish restrained from unrestrained behavior. Keep the rear structural element clear of the required equipment and storage access.

9. Generate reproducible CAD and manufacturing files

Use an editable parametric workflow, such as FreeCAD, CadQuery, or an equivalent solid-modeling tool. Keep the project in a clear source structure with one authoritative parameter set. Use millimeters internally; provide inch dimensions for review where helpful and convert exports deliberately if a supplier requires inches.

Generate and execute the model-building code or CAD workflow. Produce actual files, not unexecuted snippets or placeholder exports. Model the complete assembly, simplified equipment envelopes, feet, pads, locks, hardware, and relevant moving clearances.

Derive all part profiles, drawings, and the assembled model from the same geometry and revision. Use STEP for solid-model exchange and DXF for the supplier’s flat cutting profiles where appropriate. Mesh files or renderings are supplementary and must not replace accurate manufacturing geometry.

For each cutting file, verify closed contours, correct outer boundaries and inner openings, no duplicate or overlapping cuts, proper scale and units, valid arcs, sufficient cutter access, and absence of accidental dimension/text/toolpath geometry. Distinguish through-cuts, pockets, engraving, and drilling according to the selected supplier’s conventions. State which face is machined and any required setup change.

Reimport the exported files and compare actual bounding dimensions, holes, slots, reliefs, quantities, and orientation against the assembly. Verify that handed or mirrored parts are correct. Keep purchased hardware and equipment out of manufacturing profiles.

Generate a proposed sheet nest using actual stock dimensions, usable machine area, margins, cutter spacing, hold-down requirements, and grain constraints. Include all parts and spare small locking keys. Show sheet count, material area, waste, and the longest packed part. Do not claim the design fits one sheet from total area alone. Supply individual part files even when a combined nesting file is also useful; let the fabricator confirm its preferred nesting workflow.

10. Cleanup, finish, audio, and home testing

Distinguish furniture joinery tabs from temporary machining hold-down tabs. Specify who removes holding tabs or onion-skin remnants and sands the resulting marks. Design critical mating surfaces and visible edges accordingly. A kit with unremoved holding tabs must not be described as ready to assemble without cleanup.

Specify exposed-edge sanding or rounding, face preparation, finish choice, and treatment of mating surfaces. Prefinished plywood still has newly cut edges. If the shop supplies a coating, allow for its thickness and cure before packing. State the delivered condition precisely: raw cut parts, sanded assembly kit, or fully finished assembly kit.

Choose floor-protecting feet and a leveling method. Specify speaker isolation for actual load, contact area, compressed height, and stability. Keep speakers from touching the frame directly through unintended rigid contacts. Address loose keys, rattles, cable contact, and structural or airborne feedback without claiming the design guarantees isolation.

Provide a staged home inspection and test: inspect parts, check the fit coupon if supplied, assemble and level, verify joint engagement, load progressively with appropriate inert weights before installing electronics, check deflection and movement, then install equipment and test playback gradually at normal listening levels. Include clear adjustment or stop criteria for excessive movement, poor fit, rattles, or feedback.

Physical tests are a proposed plan until someone performs them. Do not report simulated or anticipated behavior as measured prototype performance.

11. Cost verification and deliverables

Produce a complete BOM and manufacturing schedule: material product and grade, measured thickness requirement, part IDs and quantities, grain direction, machining operations, locks or hardware, feet, isolation, cleanup, finish, packing, and delivery assumptions.

Show fabrication, accessories, shipping, and tax separately. Label each amount as an actual dated quote, a supplier-listed price, or an estimate. Account for minimum order or setup charges and full-sheet purchasing where applicable. No invented prices, supplier approvals, lead times, or tested upload results.

If over budget, revise the geometry, nesting, material choice, or optional finish while preserving fit, structural adequacy, safe edges, and manageable assembly. Show the effect of each useful revision. Stop generating alternatives once a sound affordable design is sufficiently established.

Deliver an organized downloadable package containing:

1. A concise design recommendation with overall dimensions, clear openings, operating height, material, approximate weight, intended loads, assembly method, and cost status.
2. An input/assumption register and the exact outstanding measurements with instructions for taking them.
3. Editable CAD source and parameters, software/dependency versions, and reproducible generation commands.
4. An assembly STEP model, individual part DXFs, supplier-specific files, proposed sheet nest, and joint-fit coupon files.
5. Dimensioned part and assembly drawings with revision, units, material, grain direction, slots, reliefs, pocket depths if any, finish, and critical tolerances.
6. CAD-derived assembled, loaded, section, exploded, and intermediate assembly views.
7. A BOM, fabrication schedule, purchasing list, and itemized budget or quote comparison.
8. Reproducible engineering calculations and a validation report covering fit, joinery, machining, exports, assembly, structure, stability, and audio considerations.
9. Numbered assembly/disassembly instructions, tools, labeling key, leveling and cable guidance, care instructions, and the home test plan.
10. A supplier upload guide naming the exact files, material, thickness, quantities, and requested cleanup/finishing, plus sources and a revision/change log.

Use consistent part IDs, dimensions, quantities, and revisions throughout. Make the entry document obvious. Identify any missing files or uncompleted checks explicitly.

12. Independent review and release status

Have an independent agent review the actual CAD, exports, calculations, nesting, and assembly sequence if available. Ask it to seek impossible insertion paths, unsupported fit assumptions, weak tabs, grain mistakes, missing bracing, clearance conflicts, duplicate cuts, and omitted costs. Resolve significant findings and keep a short review log. If independent review is unavailable, say so and conduct a structured second pass.

Provide a final matrix listing each requirement, acceptance criterion, evidence, result, and unresolved issue. Separate analytically checked, CAD checked, supplier reviewed, coupon tested, and physically tested. Mark unperformed checks honestly.

Use a precise status: provisional design; quote-ready package; supplier-reviewed files awaiting a fit coupon; or production-ready files with the stated prerequisites satisfied. Do not release files for manufacture while critical equipment dimensions, material thickness, joint fit, structural checks, machining compatibility, or assembly paths remain unresolved. A successful CAD upload or a quote is not structural validation.

Continue useful work through the available tools and information. If a critical measurement or tool limitation prevents completion, deliver the coherent provisional package and identify the exact blocker and affected parts. Keep routine design decisions moving with documented defaults.

The intended outcome is an affordable, compact plywood stand that arrives as accurately cut, labeled parts, assembles easily, securely supports the turntable, both horizontal speakers, and record storage, and has a clear body of evidence supporting its design.
