Act as a mechanical product designer with experience in sheet-metal fabrication, furniture stability, CAD, and practical assembly. Design a compact three-level metal stand, validate it, and produce the files needed to obtain fabrication quotes and build it at home. Carry the work through to a coherent, reproducible design package. A rendering or conceptual recommendation alone is insufficient.

1. The product I want

The stand must have three usable levels:

- Top: my Audio-Technica turntable, with a modest margin around it. I want the stand’s overall footprint as close to the turntable’s footprint as practical.
- Middle: two Acoustic Research Powered Partners lying horizontally on their sides, next to one another, with their fronts facing the listener. Include appropriate speaker isolation and access to connections and controls.
- Bottom: a removable box, crate, or one or more holders for upright vinyl records. Choose storage that works within the compact footprint. Two separate crates are optional, not a requirement.

I want a custom metal design that a fabricator can cut, bend, deburr, finish, and, where appropriate, fit with threaded hardware. It should arrive ready to assemble with ordinary hand tools. Home assembly must not require welding, drilling, bending, tapping, thread chasing, filing, deburring, painting, or setting inserts.

Prioritize, in order: correct equipment and storage fit; stiffness and stability; straightforward manufacturing and home assembly; compactness; appearance and reasonable one-off cost. Keep the design visually simple, with useful access and little wasted space. A dark powder-coated finish is a reasonable default, but no finish, alloy, height, or specific construction has been finalized.

2. What is known, and what must be verified

Earlier discussion identified the turntable as an Audio-Technica AT-LP120XUSB, but that identification was introduced by an assistant and was not explicitly confirmed by me. For that model, Audio-Technica’s manual lists 452.0 mm wide × 352.0 mm deep × 141.6 mm high, and 8 kg without the dust cover. Use this as a sourced provisional reference, not confirmation of my equipment. See source [1].

The speakers were described as Acoustic Research Powered Partners. The exact version remains unconfirmed; do not substitute dimensions for the Powered Partner 570 or another variant without evidence. Earlier discussion used approximately 10.5 × 6 × 7.9 inches per speaker. These are unverified figures with potentially ambiguous axes. Establish actual width, height, and depth in the intended horizontal orientation.

An earlier cabinet suggestion was approximately 24 inches wide × 16 inches deep × 32 inches high. Those were rough suggestions, not approved dimensions. The earlier proposed shelf openings did not fully explain that overall height. Likewise, proposed M5 hardware, a particular Allen-key size, five or six panels, and 16–24 bolts were illustrative rather than requirements.

No record container, storage capacity, maximum height, or budget has been selected. Suggest sensible defaults and explain their consequences. Do not silently assume two conventional record crates fit side by side.

Start an input register with each value, units, source, verification status, and consequence if wrong. Distinguish user measurements, manufacturer specifications, and design assumptions. Use millimeters as the authoritative CAD units and show inches alongside key user-facing dimensions.

Request only the missing information that materially affects the design. Consolidate essential questions into one short list. Develop a clearly labeled provisional design using exposed parameters while awaiting answers; do not stop after asking questions. Unresolved critical dimensions must remain identified as blockers to manufacturing release.

3. Verify the equipment and storage envelopes

For the turntable, establish the model, total mass in use, body dimensions, support-foot positions and contact areas, rear connector projection, cable bend space, dust-cover hinges, and the cover’s full opening envelope. Account for hand access, cueing, and a nearby wall. All turntable feet must bear fully on a flat support surface; flange edges and screw heads must not interfere.

For each speaker, establish horizontal-orientation dimensions, weight, stable contact surfaces, existing feet, grille position, controls, vents, ports, connectors, and cable projections. Confirm that the proposed orientation does not obstruct ventilation or place the cabinet on a fragile feature. Include the loaded height and full footprint of isolation pads. Do not assume both speakers have identical rear hardware or weight.

For the records, select at least one realistic container or holder arrangement with documented external dimensions. Include handles, feet, taper, loaded records and sleeves, maximum loaded mass, insertion clearance, and room to remove or browse records. A nominal 12-inch record diameter is not an adequate container specification. If a container cannot yet be selected, define a maximum compatible external envelope and label the recommendation provisional.

Make simple equipment and container envelope models part of the CAD assembly. Show the arrangement loaded, with the dust cover open, and with the record container moving through its removal path.

4. Derive the smallest practical geometry

Resolve the width conflict explicitly. If each speaker is actually 10.5 inches wide horizontally, the pair alone needs 21 inches before gaps, side structures, hardware, or tolerances. Explain what sets the minimum width. Preserve the requested horizontal, forward-facing speaker arrangement.

Show dimension chains for:

- Speaker bay clear width: both actual speaker widths, useful separation, isolation footprint, side clearance, and any intruding hardware.
- Overall width: the largest required usable envelope plus the actual supporting structure and tolerances.
- Depth: equipment, storage, structural members, connectors, cable routing, and wall clearance.
- Speaker opening height: speaker height, compressed isolation height, handling clearance, and any upper-shelf flange intrusion.
- Record opening height: the selected loaded container and usable access, accounting for handles and structural intrusions.
- Overall height: floor clearance, feet, shelf elevations, actual sheet thicknesses, and all clear openings.

Report overall footprint, shelf surface dimensions, unobstructed clear openings, and equipment operating clearances separately. Include protruding feet or fasteners in the true footprint. Distinguish cabinet depth from the total space needed between its front and the wall.

If a narrower top over a wider speaker bay offers a useful benefit, assess it briefly. Choose a simple, coherent layout rather than complicating construction merely to reduce one surface dimension. Explain the proposed turntable operating height.

5. Select a construction and fabrication route

Briefly compare two or three practical architectures, such as folded sheet-metal shelves between formed side members, shelves on a bolted metal frame, or another simpler construction. Compare usable space, stiffness, racking resistance, manufacturing complexity, weight, shipping, and assembly. Then choose one and develop it fully.

Assess suitable stocked steel and aluminum options using actual alloy, temper where relevant, thickness, bendability, mechanical properties, finish compatibility, and cost. Do not assume that a thin metal sheet is adequately stiff without supporting geometry. Prefer effective section shape and load paths over arbitrary thickness increases.

Use SendCutSend as an initial fabrication candidate; consider OSH Cut or another documented provider if more appropriate. Research current official rules for the exact material and combination of operations. Vendor names and prior suggestions are starting points, not guarantees of service compatibility.

Verify stock thickness, inside bend radius, bend allowance or deduction, minimum flange length, hole-to-edge and hole-to-bend spacing, bend reliefs, tooling and bend-sequence access, hardware insertion access, and manufacturing tolerances. Check maximum part and flat-blank dimensions separately for cutting, bending, hardware insertion, coating, and shipping. Use the most restrictive applicable limit.

Provide an effective means of resisting lateral and front-to-back racking. Explain how it works. An unspecified rear strip or a rectangular frame with freely rotating joints is not a validated solution. Keep bracing clear of speakers, wires, and removable record storage.

Specify safe exposed edges and corners using operations the chosen supplier actually provides. Confirm any proposed hems, special folds, or cosmetic treatments rather than assuming availability.

6. Engineer the joints, finish, and home assembly

Choose a small, consistent fastener family where practical. Specify screw diameter, pitch, head style, length, material or grade, washers where needed, locking method, engagement, and the actual driver or wrench. Determine bolt count from the joint design.

If using captive or self-clinching hardware, specify exact catalog parts, installation face, compatible sheet material and thickness, edge and bend clearance, and relevant load ratings. Check pull-through, push-out, rotation, and joint load direction as applicable. Verify the supplier’s installation sequence and any automatic changes to mounting holes.

Ensure every thread arrives usable after finishing. Distinguish tapped sheet holes from factory-installed threaded hardware; confirm masking, plugging, or factory thread cleanup as necessary. The finished kit must not depend on me chasing coated threads at home.

Include coating buildup and production tolerances in clearance holes, slots, nested panels, and mating dimensions. Show a worst-case tolerance stack for critical joints and clear equipment openings. Avoid relying on perfect nominal CAD alignment. Do not make all joints loose to accommodate errors; preserve location and racking resistance deliberately.

Use suitable clamped-joint details. Do not automatically insert nylon or other creeping materials into structural joints simply to protect paint. Address loosening, coating damage, and dissimilar materials where relevant.

Model hardware envelopes, including screw heads, washer stacks, insert protrusions, and screw tips. Prove tool access and assembly order. Parts must be installable, aligned, and tightened without bending them by force, reaching through inaccessible gaps, or trapping a final fastener.

Provide replaceable, floor-protecting feet and a practical leveling method. Consider foot attachment strength and stability. Aim for comfortable assembly by one person; state if a second person is needed for a particular step. Give a realistic tool list and time estimate based on the actual design.

7. Validate strength, stiffness, and stability

Define load cases and acceptance criteria before claiming the design passes. If actual payloads are still unknown, begin with provisional service payloads of:

- 15 kg on top.
- 10 kg on the speaker shelf.
- 40 kg on the record shelf.
- The structure’s own weight in addition to these payloads.

These are design assumptions, not established user requirements or certified load ratings. Increase or revise them when actual equipment, container capacity, or other evidence warrants it.

Evaluate simultaneous full loading, individual shelf loading, concentrated turntable-foot loads, realistic speaker-pad contact loads, record-box contact points, eccentric placement, and relevant partially loaded conditions. Account for reasonable handling disturbances and container insertion or removal.

Check shelf stress and deflection; local panel or flange deformation and buckling where relevant; side members; braces; fastener tension and shear; sheet bearing and tear-out; inserts; and feet. Trace loads from each payload to the floor. Identify the governing failure or serviceability mode.

Set and justify numerical limits for shelf deflection, twist, and racking displacement, with special attention to maintaining a level turntable support. State safety factors and whether quoted hardware capacities are ultimate or allowable. As an initial static design objective, use at least a factor of two against material yield unless a better-supported method is justified. Do not treat that objective as a furniture certification or as a substitute for other failure checks.

Use transparent, reproducible calculations with cited material properties, formulas, assumptions, units, boundary conditions, and results. Include a runnable calculation script or equivalent reproducible worksheet. State where a simplified beam model does or does not represent the folded assembly and joints.

Use finite-element analysis if available and materially useful. If used, report solver, element types, mesh sensitivity, contact and joint assumptions, load application, constraints, material model, and interpretation. Avoid artificially fixing every joint or reporting singular stress peaks as meaningful margins. A colored plot alone is not validation. If FEA is unavailable, provide honest analytical checks and identify what remains unresolved.

Check rocking, sliding, and tipping independently. Evaluate the loaded stand, the stand with records removed, asymmetric storage, and any credible condition with a container partially pulled out. Use the actual support polygon and centers of mass. State the assumed horizontal disturbance, its application height, and the resulting margin or tipping threshold. Do not depend on records always being present as ballast.

Revise the geometry if stability or racking is inadequate. Explain any recommended discreet wall restraint and its effect on the installation requirements. Distinguish unrestrained stability from performance that requires an anchor.

8. Address vibration and real use

Preserve the requested shared-stand arrangement while addressing both structural vibration and airborne feedback. Select speaker isolation for the supported load, contact area, loaded height, and stability. Keep the speakers clear of rigid bridges to the frame. Do not claim arbitrary rubber pads or a heavy stand guarantee isolation.

Assess possible sheet-metal ringing, loose panels, hardware rattles, cable contact, and any useful damping treatment. Damping must tolerate the intended use and not interfere with ventilation, finish, or assembly. Keep front lips or braces from obstructing the speaker grilles. Place speakers sensibly relative to the shelf front and explain the unavoidable limits of close spacing.

Provide a staged home test: assemble and level the empty stand; verify fit; add equipment and storage; check for rattles and movement; then test playback gradually at normal listening levels. Include a clear stop-and-adjust response to feedback or instability. Do not deliberately provoke extreme feedback, strike the turntable with the stylus down, or recommend tests that endanger the equipment.

Separate a proposed test plan from completed testing. Physical acoustic performance remains unverified until an actual build is tested.

9. Build real, reproducible CAD and check the exports

Use an editable parametric solid-modeling workflow suitable for sheet metal, such as FreeCAD, CadQuery with a justified manufacturing workflow, or an equivalent tool. Centralize the equipment dimensions, gaps, shelf elevations, material thicknesses, bend parameters, and hardware locations.

Generate actual model and export files. Do not provide code that merely claims to generate them without executing it. Keep purchased hardware and equipment as separately identified assembly components.

Validate solid geometry, uniform sheet thickness, interference, hole alignment, flange orientation, hardware access, brace clearance, equipment fit, dust-cover travel, and container insertion. Inspect front, side, top, rear, underside, section, and exploded views where needed.

Generate folded STEP files and supplier-appropriate flat patterns from the same revision. If the supplier requires its own bend parameters or performs unfolding, follow that workflow and state which files are authoritative. An arbitrarily flattened outline is not a valid manufacturing pattern.

Keep full reference assemblies separate from manufacturing uploads. Exclude equipment and purchased hardware from cut-part files. Follow the vendor’s conventions for bend lines, layers, holes, quantities, and units; prevent reference lines from being interpreted as cuts. Supply one clearly identified file per manufactured part where required.

Reopen exported files and compare their dimensions, units, solids, hole locations, and part counts with the source assembly and drawings. If a supplier’s tool modifies geometry for its tooling, inspect the resulting manufactured model and recheck affected fits. Record which export and supplier checks were actually performed.

10. Cost and procurement

Prepare a complete bill of materials and fabrication schedule, including quantities, part IDs, stock material and thickness, finish, bends, installed hardware, purchased fasteners, feet, isolation, optional damping, and the selected record storage.

Estimate the total delivered cost for one stand, separating fabrication, finishing, installed hardware, purchased items, shipping, and taxes where known. Label assumptions and dated estimates. Do not invent a live quote, availability, lead time, or an upload approval.

Identify the main cost drivers and one sensible cheaper alternative if cost is high. Avoid excessive variants or optimizations that do not improve the finished stand. Provide exact quote-upload instructions and the files to use. Do not purchase anything or send messages to fabricators.

11. Deliverables

Deliver an organized package containing:

1. A concise recommendation with overall dimensions, usable openings, material, finish, approximate weight, intended payloads, cost status, and outstanding decisions.
2. The verified input and assumption register, including the few measurements still needed and how to take them.
3. Editable parametric CAD source, configuration, software versions, and instructions to regenerate the complete design.
4. An assembled STEP model, manufacturing part files, and valid vendor-specific flat patterns where appropriate.
5. Dimensioned assembly and part drawings with revision, units, material, thickness, bend direction, radii, hole locations, hardware installation, finish, and critical tolerances.
6. Clear views and an exploded assembly illustration generated from the actual CAD geometry.
7. A complete BOM, fabrication schedule, and purchasing list with verified part references where available.
8. Reproducible calculations and a validation report covering fit, loads, stiffness, joints, stability, tolerances, manufacturing, assembly, and acoustic considerations.
9. Numbered home-assembly instructions with fastener callouts, orientation, tool access, tightening guidance, leveling, cable routing, and equipment installation.
10. A practical inspection and home test plan, care guidance, source list, and change log.

Package the files for download, with an obvious entry document. Keep part numbers, quantities, revisions, and dimensions consistent throughout. Identify every missing or provisional deliverable explicitly. No empty placeholder exports or unsupported claims of completion.

12. Independent review and completion standard

If another agent is available, have it independently review the actual design, calculations, drawings, and assembly sequence. Give it the requirements and ask it to look for failure modes and contradictions. Resolve material findings and retain a short review log. If no independent reviewer is available, state that and perform a structured second pass.

Create a final validation matrix with: requirement, criterion, evidence, result, and unresolved issue. Distinguish analytically checked, CAD checked, supplier checked, and physically tested. Mark checks that were not run as not run.

Use precise completion language: provisional concept; dimensionally checked design; quote-ready package; supplier-accepted manufacturing package; or physically tested build. Do not call a design fabrication-ready while critical equipment measurements, manufacturing compatibility, load-path checks, or assembly questions remain unresolved. Supplier acceptance establishes manufacturability within its review scope; it does not certify the furniture’s structural or acoustic performance.

Carry out the available work before returning. If a critical measurement or capability prevents final release, deliver the useful provisional package and name the exact remaining input and affected parts. Keep routine decisions moving with documented defaults.

The goal is a compact metal stand I can order as finished parts, assemble easily at home, and use for the turntable, both horizontal speakers, and removable record storage, with evidence supporting its fit, strength, stability, and manufacturability.
